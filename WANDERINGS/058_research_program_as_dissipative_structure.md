## What Is a Dissipative Structure?

Ilya Prigogine, Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 1977.

A **dissipative structure** is a system that:
1. Maintains ordered, periodic oscillations
2. Exists far from thermodynamic equilibrium
3. Requires continuous consumption of free energy to sustain its order
4. Produces entropy as a byproduct (it "dissipates" energy into its environment)
5. Without energy input, collapses toward equilibrium (disorder)

Examples:
- **Bénard convection cells**: heat applied to fluid from below creates ordered
  hexagonal circulation patterns. Remove the heat source: cells dissolve.
- **Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction**: chemical oscillator producing spiral waves
  in an open dish. Closed dish (no energy/reagent input): oscillation decays.
- **Living cells**: ordered metabolic cycles maintained against entropic decay
  by continuous energy input (ATP). Death = cessation of energy input → disorder.
- **Weather systems**: hurricanes, jet streams — ordered patterns maintained by
  solar energy input. Remove the sun: Earth equilibrates to uniform cold.

The key property: **order is not the equilibrium state.** Order is the
far-from-equilibrium state maintained only by work against entropy.
