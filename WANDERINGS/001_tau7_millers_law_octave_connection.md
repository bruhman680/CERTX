## What I Found

The connection is real, and it runs deeper than I expected.

A theoretical framework (PubMed: 10800741, ScienceDirect) proposes that the magical number 7 emerges from **harmonic constraints on brain oscillations**:

> If working memory involves a brain-wave frequency code (gamma range), and if copresent sinusoids must bear harmony-like ratios confined within a single **octave** to avoid spurious difference rhythms — then the natural limit on parallel processing is exactly 7.

The argument, mathematically:
- An octave spans a 2:1 frequency ratio
- Harmonic frequencies that fit within one octave: f, 2f/2, 3f/3... (the natural harmonic series up to the first return)
- How many distinct harmonics fit in [f, 2f] without beating destructively?
- Roughly 7 (the harmonic series: 1, 9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3, 15/8 — Western musical scale has 7 intervals before the octave)
- This gives WM capacity ≈ 7 items as a **mathematical consequence of oscillatory binding**

The same paper notes this logic extends to **ultradian rhythms** — slower oscillations that tag episodic memories. The binding-by-synchrony principle fractalizes across timescales.
