## Repair with declared types
Choose an Itô model on R^d: dX_t=b(t,X_t)dt+sigma(t,X_t)dW_t, covariance a=sigma sigma^T, and densities p_t. For Y_s=X_(T-s), under appropriate regularity and density hypotheses, the candidate reverse drift in increasing s is
```
b_reverse_i(s,x) = -b_i(T-s,x)
                    + [sum_j partial_j(a_ij(T-s,x) p_(T-s)(x))]/p_(T-s)(x).
```
This contains covariance divergence and a density-score contribution, not simply div of the noise-amplitude D. The increasing reverse-time convention matters. It reverses a path law with a reverse filtration; it is neither a deterministic inverse nor an automatic stabilization procedure. As a transparent counterpressure, Brownian motion started at zero has b=0,a=1,p_t=N(0,t): the reverse drift is -x/(T-s), whereas the visible source formula would provide no drift correction. The terminal singularity also prevents casual horizon-uniform claims.

For a fixed finite undirected weighted graph define A=alpha_c L+gamma I, Phi_0 in R^n, and S in L^1([0,T];R^n). The explicitly repaired evolution map is
```
E_t:(Phi_0,S) -> exp(-t A) Phi_0 + integral_0^t exp(-(t-r)A) S(r) dr.
```
It solves dot(Phi)=-A Phi+S. The screenshot homogeneous formula applies only with absent forcing and the correct generator/time scaling. On a continuum domain use the semigroup generated by alpha_c Delta-gamma with declared boundary conditions; discretization requires its own consistency/stability claim. On an isolated node with Phi_0=0, gamma>0 and S=1, repaired Phi(t)=(1-exp(-gamma t))/gamma while the screenshot homogeneous formula gives zero. This analytic witness needs no simulated result.

A useful typed comparison is projection P:(Phi_0,S)->Phi_0 followed by homogeneous H_t:R^n->R^n versus E_t followed by identity. Their defect is the source convolution (plus any omitted decay/scaling). This is a lossy-map comparison, not a forced cube. For L symmetric positive semidefinite, alpha_c>=0 and gamma>=0, its norm is at most integral_0^t exp(-gamma(t-r))||S(r)||dr. Small instantaneous forcing therefore does not itself guarantee small long-horizon error when gamma=0.
