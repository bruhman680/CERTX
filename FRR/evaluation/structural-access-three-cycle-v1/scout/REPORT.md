## Primary methods read

[Bolukbasi et al., NIPS 2016](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/a486cd07e4ac3d270571622f4f316ec5-Paper.pdf), sections 2–4, especially section 3's paired differences and section 4's complete Identify gender subspace step. They assume defining word pairs/sets and estimate a subspace from variation within those sets. Ten paired comparisons motivate a leading component; the generalized algorithm forms a sum of within-set centered outer products and takes a specified number of singular directions. The pair/defining-set choices and dimension parameter are supplied inputs. The application is static word-embedding geometry. This is a supervised contrast analogue, not evidence for learning negation, scopes or uncertainty in the fixture, nor a guarantee of complete factor recovery.

[Yu, Wang and Samworth, arXiv:1405.0680v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0680), sections 1–2, including the complete statement, conditions and discussion of Theorem 2. This is the accessible 2014 preprint of the work published in Biometrika 102(2), 315–323 (2015), DOI 10.1093/biomet/asv008. For symmetric population and estimated matrices, separated target eigenvalues yield a principal-angle error bound controlled by their matrix difference and the population eigengap. For a top-k subspace with gap g = lambda_k - lambda_(k+1) > 0, the theorem gives ||sin Theta||_F <= 2 min(sqrt(k)||E||_op, ||E||_F)/g. The bound concerns subspaces and allows an orthogonal alignment of eigenvector bases. It does not itself establish a noise concentration bound or a sufficient training sample count. The initially surfaced UCL author PDF returned 404; the arXiv full text supplied the theorem reading.

## Consequences for this contact: our algebra and design inference

**Known access versus transported access.** For a common orthogonal Q, inner products are unchanged. If P is the binding projector, the same supplied access in transformed coordinates is P_Q = Q P Q^T, since P_Q Qx = QPx. Leaving P fixed can change the quantity read out. These are deterministic identities, not empirical learning claims. They do not depend on either paper and need no semantic attribution.

**What paired training identifies.** Let a matched pair be x_i^+ = c_i + B z_i and x_i^- = c_i - B z_i with orthonormal columns B. Then d_i = x_i^+ - x_i^- = 2 B z_i cancels the shared nuisance. Estimate an explicitly chosen rank from D = [d_1,...,d_m], equivalently from S = (1/m) sum_i d_i d_i^T. For a two-element defining set, its within-set scatter is d_i d_i^T/4, so its span agrees with this construction. This is the narrow methodological analogy to the first source.

Do not subtract the mean of the difference columns without a declared reason. Globally centering D is a different estimator: identical nonzero contrasts would become zero, and its rank is at most m-1. Pair-centering cancels each pair's common context while retaining its contrast. Contrast orientation changes do not affect the uncentered outer-product estimator.

**Rank and sample limits.** In zero noise, rank(D) <= min(m,k), where k is the true binding dimension. Exact full-subspace recovery requires rank([z_1,...,z_m]) = k. Thus m >= k is necessary, not sufficient. Distinct training entities prevent direct entity overlap; they do not establish spanning or generalization. Opposite-status supervision alone may expose only a proper subspace. A truncated rank-k estimate with fewer than k supported directions includes arbitrary completion directions; it must not be described as recovered structure. Separate assumed target rank, numerical rank and fitted rank. The projector identifies a span, not a unique coordinate basis or status decoder.

**Noise and pairing limits.** With additive residuals, d_i = 2Bz_i + eta_i. Independent zero-mean isotropic pair noise shifts the population second moment by a multiple of the identity and preserves its signal eigenspace; finite-sample perturbation still matters. Shared noise may cancel, and independent equal-variance noise doubles difference variance. Anisotropic noise or unmatched pair contexts can change the population eigenspace and create systematic error, so more samples alone need not repair identification. The second source explains why a weak or missing eigengap prevents a useful generic perturbation guarantee. Applying its theorem to this estimator also requires establishing a bound on the actual matrix perturbation under the specified pair/noise distribution; no universal sample threshold follows here.

**Suggested frozen reporting.** Predeclare pair generation, training/test entity separation, target rank, rank tolerance, sample/noise grid and unavailable/rank-deficient behavior before outcomes. Evaluate estimated P_hat against P_Q through projector distance or principal angles when oracle access is available for evaluation. Keep this structural metric separate from held-out readout success. P_Q is privileged structural access; P_hat uses paired labels and a cancellation model. Neither establishes unsupervised semantic discovery. Test sets must not choose the rank or tolerance.

The complete pair-difference estimator is orthogonally equivariant at the subspace/projector level when rank and tie handling select a unique subspace: S_Q = Q S Q^T. A tie across the truncation boundary removes uniqueness; arbitrary singular-vector bases within an otherwise fixed selected subspace are harmless to its projector. This distinction prevents a basis mismatch from being mistaken for information loss.
