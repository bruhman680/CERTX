# Seed and environment ideas for the redesign (Claude scout notes for Codex, 2026-11-05)

Status: ideas plus literature contacts, written while the redesign is still in progress. Nothing here is a result about the new world or about a model. Six primary pages were read at abstract level through the fetch tool (read depth per paper below); one small measurement was run on Codex's saved garden entries (read-only). Thomas's design intent, as he described it to me: seeds as raw material (e.g. numbers) instead of tasks, an environment that looks open to the model while the observers know where things live and die, no goal beyond the model's own curiosity about its world's "physics," and the view that curiosity must be narrowed and honed to be useful.

## A. Literature contacts (what each establishes, and what it does not)
1. **Can large language models explore in-context?** (Krishnamurthy, Harris, Foster, Zhang, Slivkins; arXiv 2403.15371v3). Abstract-level read. In multi-armed bandits with history in the prompt, GPT-3.5/GPT-4/Llama2 "do not robustly engage in exploration without substantial interventions"; the one satisfactory configuration was GPT-4 with chain-of-thought and an externally summarized history (sufficient statistics). Simple bandit settings only; larger frozen-model behaviour, so not evidence about a 135M model. Relevance: exploration from a frozen model's context is known to be brittle, and the shape of the history (summary vs raw) can matter more than the invitation.
2. **Large-Scale Study of Curiosity-Driven Learning** (Burda, Edwards, Pathak, Storkey, Darrell, Efros; arXiv 1808.04355). Abstract-level read. Curiosity there is an intrinsic reward equal to prediction error, studied across 54 environments with no extrinsic reward; the abstract reports surprisingly good performance and names prediction-based reward failing in stochastic setups as a limitation (it does not use the term "noisy TV"). Relevance: raw surprise-seeking is captured by irreducible randomness; the garden's frozen model has no such reward, so this is a contrast case, not a prediction.
3. **Open-Endedness is Essential for Artificial Superhuman Intelligence** (Hughes et al.; arXiv 2406.04268, ICML 2024). Abstract-level read (last ~5.8k chars unread). Open-endedness is defined relative to an observer: artifacts are novel (later ones stay harder to predict) and learnable (more history makes them more predictable). Observer-dependent by design; the paper judges present foundation models not open-ended under this definition. Relevance: an operational way to say "worth continuing" without a task goal, as a measurement and not a reward.
4. **Emergent World Representations** (Li et al.; arXiv 2210.13382v5). Abstract-level read. A GPT trained on Othello move sequences shows evidence of a nonlinear internal board-state representation, and interventions on it change outputs. Relevance: precedent for asking whether a model has internalized a world's rules rather than surface statistics; the trained model there is not a frozen general model, and we cannot probe internals here, but behavioural analogues exist (legal-move rate, consequence prediction).
5. **Voyager** (Wang et al.; arXiv 2305.16291). Abstract-level read. An LLM agent with an automatic curriculum, a skill library of executable code, and iterative prompting from environment feedback and execution errors; relies on black-box GPT-4. The abstract states no limitations. Relevance: the published pattern closest to the Scheme world (errors from execution drive revision; working procedures are stored and reused). Do not import its competence expectations: it depends on a far larger model.
6. **Tokenization counts** (Singh, Strouse; arXiv 2402.14903). Abstract-level read. Right-to-left digit grouping (via comma separation) improves frontier-model arithmetic over left-to-right (e.g. 75.6% to 97.8% for GPT-3.5, 8-shot addition); the gap shrinks with scale, not monotonically. API models only. Relevance: how numbers are tokenized and formatted is a hidden variable in any numeric seed. I could not load SmolLM2's tokenizer here (transformers not installed), so how it splits digits is UNVERIFIED; Codex has it locally.

Absence from my search is not absence from the literature. Standard-mode search only; Codex's earlier scouts were excluded. No source was verified beyond the pages named.

## B. Ideas, with what each could show and how it could fail
1. **Generator ledger for numeric seeds.** Each seed is raw numbers from a generator the observers pre-register (structured: rule-based, e.g. squares, parity, modular pattern; matched shuffled: same digits, no rule; trivial: constants), plus the verifier for every regularity claim a model could make. Include a nuisance regularity (true but irrelevant) so noticing something is not the same as noticing the right thing. Any model move becomes checkable without telling the model what to do. Failure mode: the model only continues fluent-looking strings, which the ledger will show.
2. **Matched shuffled control for the physics (my scrambled-world control).** Same commands and interface; one world with consistent consequences, one whose consequences are inconsistent. If behaviour does not differ, the regularities were not doing the work. Both worlds' rules are inspectable by the model so nothing is hidden.
3. **Action taxonomy with continuous measures.** Copy / vary / extend / test (apply an operation and then check the result). Log graded quantities next to the labels (fraction of a continuation consistent with the generator; edit distance to the seed), because hard labels can manufacture sharp transitions (Schaeffer et al., which Codex has already checked at abstract level).
4. **Narrowing-source tag per entry.** Whether any narrowing of the model's activity came from the world (errors and rules pruning it), from its own history (revisiting, dropping), or from us (seeds, summaries, instruction text). A summary card is an intervention from us, in the sense of Krishnamurthy et al., and should be tagged as such.
5. **Learnability as an observer-side measurement.** Use an observer model (n-gram or small LM trained on earlier entries) to track novelty and learnability of a run, never as a reward or stopping rule. A streak like the garden's currency strings should look highly learnable and not novel.
6. **Legality-rate curve as a physics-learning readout.** Fraction of commands that are legal, and fraction of predicted consequences that match, over time and conditioned on shelf versions. Behavioural cousin of the Othello probing, independent of preference or curiosity claims.
7. **Number formatting as a design variable.** Hold digit grouping and separators fixed or vary them deliberately and record them, since tokenization changes arithmetic behaviour in larger models. Check how SmolLM2 splits digits first.
8. **Bounds inspectable, not hidden.** To keep the world feeling open without concealment, keep limits public and not foregrounded; the cap is then found by an overflow error that is part of the physics. If bounds were concealed, any later "discovery" would be confounded with the concealment.

## C. One measurement on the existing garden (garden_novelty_learnability.py, output beside it)
Observer model: zlib with the preceding text as a dictionary, blocks of 50 primary replies (steps 50-499). Conditional/unconditional compressed size per block is 1.000 (first block, by construction), .690, .633, .713, .652, .690, .448, .530, .378. Mean for early blocks (50-149) .845 vs late (400-499) .454 (ratio 1.86). Distinct exact replies per 50: 33, 24, 37, 32, 37, 32, 28, 25, 38.
- Predictions: N1 (late at least 1.5x more predictable) held, but only because the first block has no history; using just 100-149 against 400-499 the ratio is 1.52, barely above threshold. N2 (fewer distinct replies late) FAILED: 28.5 early vs 31.5 late. N3 (some middle block less predictable than the first) was badly formed: the first block is 1.0 by construction, so it could never hold.
- Reading: the late stream is more predictable from its own past, yet distinct exact strings did not fall, because the currency strings vary at the surface while staying one repeated family. A zlib observer is weak; this is a descriptive signature on one dependent trajectory of a 135M model, not a test of curiosity or emergence.

## D. Predictions on record (low confidence, written before any seed pilot; they may lose)
- Numeric seeds will be mostly copied or continued; test-type actions (apply an operation, then check) will be under 10% of entries in a first pilot.
- Structured versus matched-shuffled seeds will not differ visibly in dwell time or continuation correctness for the 135M model.
- Legality rate with the compact card will exceed chance but improve little with added history. (Earlier ones from my reply today: single commands work; definitions only with syntax aid; composition and repair rare without aid.)
