## Main finding

Fresh validation inputs do not necessarily challenge a shared assumption. In this toy, aligned context contamination produces an exactly recoverable contrast subspace. The estimator preserves256separate validation contrasts almost perfectly at every training prefix, yet recovers a different subspace from the intended binding target. Some downstream task settings succeed and others fail persistently. More examples of the same mechanism do not resolve that distinction.

Independent mismatch behaves differently depending on its strength. When binding variation dominates the population second moment, larger fitting prefixes approach the intended target in this grid. When nuisance dominates, larger prefixes approach the population context target while moving farther from intended binding. These are different optimization targets, rather than one universal claim that data helps or harms.

## Population targets: an explicit upstream distinction

Let d=12 and intended P_B=diag(0,I). Unrotated population second moments and their unique top-d projectors are:

| Pair mechanism | Second moment | Population target | Target distance from P_B |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Clean | diag(0,I)/(2d) | P_B | 0 |
| Independent,delta<1 | diag(delta²I,I)/(2d) | P_B | 0 |
| Independent,delta>1 | diag(delta²I,I)/(2d) | P_C=I-P_B | 1 |
| Aligned | [[delta²I,delta I],[delta I,I]]/(2d) | graph projector G_delta | delta/sqrt(1+delta²) |

Here G_delta=[[delta²I,delta I],[delta I,I]]/(1+delta²). Distance is ||P_hat-P_target||_F/sqrt(2d). The independent population gap is abs(1-delta²)/(2d); aligned gap is(1+delta²)/(2d). No degenerate delta1condition is tested. A stable, well-separated population target need not be the intended binding target.

For aligned contrasts with spanning binding samples, every m>=12recovers G_delta in exact arithmetic. Its persistent distances are.447214 at delta.5 and.832050 at delta1.5. The target is not made correct by a larger sample. We rotate all matrices consistently with the same known Q. These are model-derived identities; the finite run checks their realization, not their universal applicability.

### Undefined phase, collision and policy are different

Under G_delta the relevant clean products reduce to graph coordinates q=delta*r*c+b and w_N=delta*r*c-b. Their unnormalized product is(delta*r)²-1: real cross terms cancel, independent of c·b. At delta.5,r2 this is zero even though both vectors are usually nonzero. In the actual run every such N calibration product is invalid; the all-centres policy globally abstains on all768estimated query rows for that arm/r across four prefixes and both noise levels. This is not erasure of the whole vector or an A/N phase collision.

At delta1.5,r2 the N product is positive, so its angle collides with A at0. Ambiguous-centre abstention remains a separate reason. Another128query rows in independent1.5,r.5,m48,seeds92501/92509 have unavailable calibration too; those finite-sample centre failures are not automatically the same mechanism as the exact graph orthogonality.

Across all returned items:55,332ok reasons,11,100ambiguous-centre abstentions,2,688calibration-unavailable abstentions. No returned zero_product reason occurs because the global unavailable-centre policy takes priority in the exact graph-zero case. A different declared decoder policy could change these counts; this run did not compare policies or tune thresholds after failure.
