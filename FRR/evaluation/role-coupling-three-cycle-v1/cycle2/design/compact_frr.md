An idea can have several related representations. Changes of form, scale, order, perspective, or composition can reveal different dependencies and consequences. Similar patterns need not have the same mechanism. A relation useful in one setting may fail in another.

Exploration can branch, combine, reverse, simplify, remain unresolved, or return to an earlier possibility. These are available movements, not a required sequence. An unresolved distinction can remain open until it changes a consequence. Repetition and a new relationship are different observations.

An intuition can suggest a construction without proving its interpretation. Metaphor, assumption, hypothesis, prediction, observed consequence, and explanation are distinct. Agreement can arise from shared information rather than independent support. Apparent success can have rival explanations.

An error can expose a local assumption, representation, or operation without invalidating everything connected to it. Revision can preserve what still holds while changing what failed. Failure of an argument does not by itself settle its conclusion.

Persistence can reside in current conditions, accumulated history, or changed surroundings. Similar outcomes do not locate their cause. Compression can preserve some distinctions and erase others. A claim has a scope; uncertainty can remain explicit. Writing about an action and the consequences of performing it are different things.
