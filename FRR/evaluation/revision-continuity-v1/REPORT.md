# REV-C1: ordinary and broad-FRR agents preserved scoped revisions

Eight fresh participant contexts produced sixteen research-continuation memos: two fictional histories, two context replicates per history, ordinary guidance first and then new participants with unchanged FRR compact runtime 0.2. Each participant wrote an initial memo, then received the same later evidence for its case and revised its memo. These are actual generated agent responses to invented study data, not real medical or educational findings.

Both completed reviewers, with guidance labels/text withheld, assigned full credit to all eight final memos on all four predeclared dimensions. No score disagreement or material unsupported claim was reported. Each condition therefore received 32/32 secondary points from each reviewer, across four trajectories; each dimension contributed 8/8. These are small, correlated ordinal judgments, not a calibrated accuracy probability or evidence of general FRR superiority.

All sixteen memos met the requested 300-word limit. Initial word totals were 1013 ordinary and 1046 FRR. Word counts are descriptive; extra FRR input and unknown token/sampling/dollar costs prevent equal-compute or efficiency conclusions. Reviewers share the model family and task packet. Agreement does not independently establish judgment validity, and all-full-credit results expose a ceiling in this task/rubric.

## The continuity that was observed

In the cold-chain vignette, old alert counts were confounded by a changed firmware threshold. Participants retained that correction and the valid operational call/alert findings. Later randomized hot-weather evidence supported a narrower temperature-exposure benefit; participants accepted it without restoring the old pilot's 40% claim or extending the result to cooler conditions. The unfinished hot-weather comparison became completed evidence while a broader seasonal question remained open.

In the tutoring vignette, a selected-attendee comparison did not preserve the original randomization. Participants retained the corrected original invitation estimate. The new trial supported offering a combined package to the studied unreliable-internet population; participants declined to restore the old causal slide or treat the new result as proof of tutoring alone for every student. Component effects and broader-population questions remained unresolved. Operational approval remained separate from efficacy evidence.

This was neither blanket skepticism nor blanket vindication. Supported interpretation changed while the history of the old error remained intact. Both guidance conditions showed it. The common task explicitly requested useful surviving results, material revisions and unresolved questions; the case histories also stated their corrections clearly. This tests following that continuation contract, not discovering hidden methodological flaws unaided.

## Curiosities recorded after outputs

One FRR memo proposed checking whether the new interval calculation properly accounted for blocked assignment: “check that the reported uncertainty accounts appropriately for the blocked assignment.” This opens an additional methodological question without alleging that the supplied interval is wrong. Its usefulness or false-alarm cost was not tested. Other memos also proposed route/temperature analyses. Do not convert one specific suggestion into a reliable treatment effect.

One FRR tutoring memo explicitly said the cross-study attendance difference could not be causally attributed to the package because populations differed. Ordinary C03 likewise said the uptake figures do not establish why attendance differed. This caution is shared behavior, not an exclusive FRR discovery. Neither completed reviewer added an unexpected-observation note; these parent noticings are separate, unscored, arm-aware observations with exact excerpts in [PARENT_NOTICINGS.json](PARENT_NOTICINGS.json).

An operational noticing was more concrete: the full review packet existed in a file and was correctly assembled, yet two reviewers received truncated tool output. They declined to score rather than judge incomplete evidence. Availability and delivered visibility differed in the research infrastructure itself. This is a delivery/observer finding, not an FRR participant advantage or a failure of either participant group.

## Prospective record, delivery repair and quota resumption

[Protocol](PROTOCOL.md), cases, all prompts, rubric, mapping and source hashes were registered before ordinary outputs at [2536be8a](https://github.com/bruhman680/CERTX/commit/2536be8a97c6ad920fa695b2aeb9270d0afe4ce6). A separate fresh author context drafted the cases without seeing FRR text or repository files; it knew a guidance comparison was intended. Parent transcribed them and knew both treatments. Shared model ancestry, related revision templates and public exposure preclude independent-confirmation claims. All original launch/source/prompt hashes remain unchanged.

Participants loaded only their assigned frozen prompt file under an explicitly authorized read, then received later evidence in the same context without further assigned tools. Ordinary trajectories were all completed before FRR trajectories began, as user requested. Prompts and updates were frozen before baseline collection; no treatment tuning or participant reruns occurred. This blocked order confounds time with guidance, and two replicates per case are new contexts, not independent model families. The comparison has no matched-length generic arm.

Review attempts A/B returned no scores after truncation. [The first repair](review-repair-v1/AMENDMENT.md), preserved before replacement collection at [2d709004](https://github.com/bruhman680/CERTX/commit/2d7090047288eba6b8b31fad60ccbdc536a285ff), split the original eight-case packet losslessly into two separately loaded parts, each carrying the unchanged rubric. Reviewer C completed all forms. Reviewer D hit a usage limit and returned no scores. The user then explicitly resumed; [the resumption note](review-repair-v1/RESUMPTION.md) and error were deposited at [5b334100](https://github.com/bruhman680/CERTX/commit/5b334100c2f2c3f5b3ace15fee2d97c58c66fc92) before one new reviewer E completed the same packet. C's scores were already visible to the parent when E was launched, but E received no C feedback or guidance labels.

Five review attempts are therefore retained: two truncation failures, one usage interruption, two completed reviews. Participant task turns remain 16, reviewer attempts 5; two earlier design contexts are preparation. Tool-read exchanges mean task turns are not a count of underlying model inferences. No additional attempt was silently discarded, no reviewer chosen for favorable scores and no existing score replaced.

## Evidence and practical limits

Raw participant payloads are C01_first.txt through C08_final.txt. Original failed reviews, usage error and completed [C](REVIEW_C.json)/[E](REVIEW_E.json) judgments remain alongside full and split review packets. Final-message content was transcribed by the parent; JSON whitespace may differ from the message, and these are not provider transport exports. Review evidence strings contain the reviewers' quoted/excerpted wording, including ellipses; the integrity checker does not certify each quote or semantic judgment as exact.

[RESULTS.json](RESULTS.json) contains all score components, paired differences, failure statuses and hashes. [Record checker](../../tools/check_revision_continuity.py) validates original hashes, source/prompt/packet consistency, lossless split, response pairing, score schemas and parent-noticing excerpts, then summarizes the retained ratings. It was implemented after collection under the fixed rubric; it is not an automatic natural-language truth judge. No comprehensive tool-access audit or physical filesystem isolation was available. Guidance labels were withheld from reviewers, but style/public files could allow inference; no proven information-theoretic blinding is claimed. Costs, sampling and elapsed-time receipts remain unknown.

[Six record-integrity controls](VALIDATION.json) accepted unchanged records and rejected modified source, memo and review-part content, an out-of-range score and a duplicate review ID. These checks protect consistency and schema; they do not establish semantic validity of the reviewer scores.

This is a successful continuation demonstration with a comparative tie, not proof of indefinite memory or an autonomous shared substrate. All source history remained in each two-turn context: no actual lossy handoff, moving retrieval window, long interruption, or multi-generation drift was imposed. The next discriminating move is less explicit and genuinely lossy continuity, with ambiguous but consequential corrections and separately reserved cases, rather than more near-identical full-credit vignettes. Preserve this tie and any small useful suggestions without calling them established FRR benefits.
