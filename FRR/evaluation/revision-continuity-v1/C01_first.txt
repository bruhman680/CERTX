The pilot supports a decline in recorded operational alerts, from 30/120 to 18/120, and independently confirmed alert-related dispatch calls, from 43 to 27. These remain useful operational findings, but they do not establish that insulation reduced the prespecified temperature-exposure endpoint.

The original claim of 40% fewer excursions requires withdrawal because installation coincided with a firmware change that lengthened the alert threshold from two to three consecutive minutes above 8°C. The safety officer authorized this change for dispatch operations; that authorization neither changed the research endpoint nor established that two-minute excursions were harmless. The dashboard’s “validated insulation benefit” statement should therefore be corrected to reflect the revised memo.

Among shipments with recoverable minute-level records, applying the original endpoint yields 16/80 before installation and 15/80 afterward. These results do not demonstrate a substantial reduction. They also do not establish equivalence or rule out benefit: missing records disproportionately involve older devices, limiting generalization, and the before-and-after comparison cannot isolate insulation’s contribution from other changes.

A possible benefit on hot routes remains unresolved because such routes were uncommon and no suitable hot-weather comparison was available. The next analysis should examine record availability by device age, period and route, and assess how plausible outcomes among missing shipments would affect the endpoint comparison. The drafted prospective study should then be completed using independent loggers and concurrent randomized comparisons, with adequate hot-weather coverage and the original two-minute endpoint retained. Operational alerts and dispatch calls should continue to be reported separately.
