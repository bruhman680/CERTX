The completed randomized study provides strong evidence that insulation reduces the prespecified endpoint on qualifying hot-route days. Independently calibrated loggers recorded at least two consecutive minutes above 8°C in 12/100 insulated shipments versus 29/100 standard shipments: a 17-percentage-point absolute reduction, with an estimated risk ratio of 0.41 (95% CI 0.22–0.76). Randomization within route-day blocks, complete records and masked logger processing strengthen this conclusion.

This resolves the earlier uncertainty about benefit in hot-route conditions. It does not validate the original pilot’s claimed 40% reduction. That claim relied on operational alerts whose threshold changed during installation. The safety officer’s description of the new study as “confirmation of the original pilot” therefore overstates continuity between the findings. Operational authorization neither amended the research endpoint nor established that shorter excursions were harmless.

The pilot’s useful findings remain: recorded alerts fell from 30/120 to 18/120, and independently confirmed dispatch calls fell from 43 to 27. Recoverable records using the original endpoint showed 16/80 before and 15/80 afterward; selective missingness limits their interpretation.

Replace the dashboard statement with: “Randomized hot-route study: two-minute temperature excursions occurred in 12% of insulated shipments versus 29% of standard shipments; estimated relative reduction 59% (95% CI 24%–78%).” Identify the original pilot’s 40% exposure claim as withdrawn.

There are no new cool-weather data, so the hot-route result should not be generalized to all shipments. A feasible next step is a concurrent randomized comparison on cooler route-days using the same independent logging and prespecified endpoint, followed by an assessment of how benefits vary with temperature and the distributor’s route mix.
