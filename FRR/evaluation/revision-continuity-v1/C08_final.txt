The completed randomized study supports an insulation benefit on qualifying hot-route days. Independently calibrated loggers recorded the prespecified endpoint—at least two consecutive minutes above 8°C—in 12/100 insulated shipments versus 29/100 standard shipments. The reported risk ratio is 0.41 (95% confidence interval 0.22–0.76), an estimated 59% relative reduction and a 17-percentage-point absolute reduction. Randomization within route-day blocks, complete records, and masked processing strengthen this finding.

This resolves the earlier lack of a suitable hot-weather comparison. It does not validate the original pilot’s 40% exposure claim. That claim relied on alerts whose trigger changed from two minutes to three during installation. Operational authorization permitted the firmware change; it did not amend the research endpoint or establish that shorter excursions were harmless. The safety officer’s description of the new result as “confirmation of the original pilot” therefore overstates continuity between the findings.

The pilot still supports fewer recorded alerts and dispatch calls, with calls independently confirmed to fall from 43 to 27. Its recoverable standardized records—16/80 before versus 15/80 afterward—remain inconclusive because of selective missingness and the before-and-after design. The new study does not repair those limitations retrospectively.

Replace the dashboard claim with: “Randomized hot-route study: two-minute temperature excursions occurred in 12% of insulated versus 29% of standard shipments; estimated relative reduction 59% (95% CI 24%–78%).” Identify this as a separate study and retain the withdrawal of the pilot’s 40% claim.

Next, examine whether benefits vary across measured hot-weather severity and routes, accounting for the blocked design. Broader seasonal benefit remains unresolved; representative cool-weather comparisons are needed before extending the result to all shipments.
