# Kestrel research cooperative: internal working history

These are fictional premises stipulated for this exercise. Each numbered record is an authoritative report of what it states; estimates, missing measurements and unresolved explanations remain limited as written. Dates are in the fictional year 2031. The cooperative studies four unrelated systems. Project labels and nearby dates do not imply shared samples, mechanisms or permissions.

## W01 — Water / January 8
The Brook sensor project measures nitrate in irrigation channels. Twenty-four sealed units, batch Fern, completed a twelve-day bench trial in clear water at 18–22°C. Median absolute error was 0.7 mg/L against a laboratory reference. The bench protocol recorded readings every hour, but its error statistic used daily noon samples only. A proposed orchard deployment has not yet begun. Its purchase worksheet lists thirty units, including six unbuilt spares.

## B01 — Battery / January 10
The Sable coating team tested twelve coin cells with recipe K and twelve untreated controls. At 25°C, recipe K cells retained a median 88% capacity after 200 cycles; controls retained 81%. The cells used laboratory electrolyte L. No production pouch cell had been assembled. Staff recorded coating time and scrap separately because a good electrochemical result would not establish a viable manufacturing process.

## C01 — Clinic / January 13
The Harbor scheduling study enrolled adult patients at two urban clinics. Its initial primary outcome was completed appointments within fourteen days of a referral. The team randomized weekly blocks to a reminder call or the usual text. Emergency referrals and patients requiring an interpreter were outside enrollment. A clinic manager proposed extending calls to all referrals, but that proposal did not change the study population or authorize any service change.

## A01 — Archive / January 16
The Lantern archive project compares OCR systems on shipping logs. An initial set contained 300 printed English pages dated 1910–1920, all from the west warehouse. The first score measured character accuracy after staff removed torn margins from the reference text. The archive also owns handwritten ledgers and French manifests. Their presence in storage did not make them members of the evaluated set. Red cover labels identified scanning order only.

## W02 — Water / February 2
Eight Fern units were placed in a greenhouse channel for seven days. Seven returned complete noon readings; the eighth lost power on day four. The published 0.9 mg/L error figure excludes that unit after its last successful reading. Nobody attributed its power loss to nitrate exposure. Greenhouse staff logged temperatures of 19–24°C and did not log turbidity. The test report calls this a continuity problem requiring investigation, without naming a cause.

## B02 — Battery / February 5
An independent technician repeated recipe K on another eight coin cells using electrolyte L. Their median retention was 86% after 200 cycles at 25°C. Two cells developed gas pockets after cycle 170; both remained included in retention statistics. The technician did not classify the pockets as a thermal event, and no abuse or puncture test was conducted. The funding committee requested a later decision memo separating cycling performance, handling safety and production yield.

## C02 — Clinic / February 9
At the first clinic, calls produced 64 completed appointments among 100 enrolled referrals, compared with 57 among 100 for texts. At the second clinic, the counts were 43 of 80 and 45 of 80. These are completed fourteen-day outcomes, not attendance among people who answered the phone. The interim analyst declined to pool them before checking site differences. The receptionist's tally of answered calls was retained only for workload planning.

## A02 — Archive / February 12
System Reed achieved 97.4% character accuracy on the original printed English set; system Slate achieved 96.9%. Reed took nine processing hours and Slate took six on the same workstation. Staff corrected references before scoring both systems. The processing clock excludes scanning and manual correction. The team selected Reed for a demonstration because the original objective ranked character accuracy first. This selection did not constitute approval for unattended archival transcription.

## W03 — Water / February 21
The orchard consortium's initial acceptance rule requires median absolute noon error at or below 1.0 mg/L for ten consecutive days in clear irrigation water. Greenhouse results cover only seven days, so the consortium requested a longer trial. An engineering note says the bench interval could support component selection, but the consortium's rule specifically requires field operation. A courier delivered fresh reference bottles; their blue caps distinguish transport batches, not sensor treatments.

## B03 — Battery / February 25
Production engineers coated a trial strip using recipe K. Of fifty punched electrodes, forty-two passed a visual uniformity screen. An earlier planning estimate assumed forty-eight would pass. The rejected eight were not assembled into cells, so neither their retention nor their safety was measured. Engineers suspected a drying gradient but had not tested that explanation. The project accountant classified the strip as development expense rather than recoverable stock.

## C03 — Clinic / March 1
A registry correction removed four duplicate referrals from the first clinic's call arm: two had completed within fourteen days and two had not. Its corrected count is therefore 62 of 96. No records changed in the text arm or second clinic. The correction did not remove people for failing to answer a call. The team asked the analyst to preserve both original and corrected tables so later descriptions could state which denominator they used.

## A03 — Archive / March 4
The archive added a distinct evaluation set of 120 printed French pages, each with an intact margin. Reed reached 91.2% character accuracy and Slate 94.0%. Staff had not evaluated handwritten pages. French results were filed separately from the original English comparison; nobody computed a joint accuracy figure. A scanner lamp replacement occurred between sets, but the report neither attributes the difference to that replacement nor rules it out as relevant.

## W04 — Water / March 11
Ten Fern units completed an eleven-day orchard trial in clear water. The daily noon reference comparisons yielded median absolute error of 0.8 mg/L, with all units reporting each noon. Three units missed overnight readings on two nights. Under the existing noon-based criterion, the consortium accepted this trial. The team did not calculate an all-hours error statistic. Rainfall remained below two millimeters and workers recorded no visible sediment during this interval.

## B04 — Battery / March 15
The committee's first funding gate requires median retention of at least 85% at 200 cycles in coin cells at 25°C, plus a written scale-up plan. Recipe K meets the cycling part in both reports. A plan was submitted, so the committee approved a small engineering grant. Its letter explicitly withholds any claim that pouch cells, cold operation or production yield meet a requirement. Grant approval does not waive separate handling procedures.

## C04 — Clinic / March 19
The hospital board changed its prospective service target to completed appointments within seven days, starting with referrals received April 1. The study's existing fourteen-day outcomes would remain valid for its original research question. Staff had referral and completion dates, but nobody had yet derived seven-day counts from them. A board member predicted calls would help the new target; that prediction was recorded as an expectation, without a numerical estimate or new authorization.

## A04 — Archive / March 22
An archivist inspected twenty Reed transcriptions selected because they contained damaged headings. Six omitted a date entirely. The sample was deliberately targeted and cannot estimate the omission rate across all 300 English pages. No comparable Slate inspection was performed. The archivist recommended a date check before publication. That recommendation identifies a possible failure mode without reversing either system's reported character accuracy on the original evaluation set.

## W05 — Water / April 3
The consortium revised its next deployment gate after a sediment blockage at another supplier's site. Future approval now requires error at or below 1.0 mg/L at every sampled hour over ten days, including turbid water above 40 NTU. Prior orchard acceptance remains in the record under the old rule. The blockage was not observed in Fern devices. The procurement officer paused new orders pending evidence addressing the revised gate, including its water condition.

## B05 — Battery / April 6
Four recipe K pouch cells were assembled with electrolyte M, selected for the intended product. After 100 cycles at 25°C, median retention was 90%. The test stopped for a fixture replacement scheduled by the lab; no cell failure caused that stop. These results are neither 200-cycle measurements nor a replication using electrolyte L. The safety officer continued to require a ventilated enclosure for charging because the available tests did not justify relaxing that procedure.

## C05 — Clinic / April 10
The analyst derived seven-day counts for the corrected historic cohort: the first clinic had 39 of 96 in the call arm and 42 of 100 in the text arm; the second had 29 of 80 and 31 of 80. These figures concern referrals enrolled before April 1. Staff have not supplied outcomes for referrals entering under the new service target. The analyst cautioned that describing these counts as an April implementation result would misdate the population.

## A05 — Archive / April 13
For a public genealogy release, the director proposed a different gate: every released page must retain all personal names and dates, or receive manual correction before release. Aggregate character accuracy alone does not certify that condition. No exhaustive names-and-dates audit has been completed for either system. A separate exhibition poster may use manually checked excerpts. Poster permission does not authorize release of the complete automated transcription corpus.

## W06 — Water / April 20
Six Fern units ran for three days in a tank at 45–55 NTU. Noon error had a median of 0.9 mg/L. Hourly reference sampling failed because the technician's reference pump jammed; sensor readings themselves continued. The team retained only noon comparisons as valid paired measurements. Investigators could not estimate all-hours error from unpaired readings. This test supplies some turbid-water evidence, but neither its duration nor reference coverage matches the revised consortium gate.

## B06 — Battery / April 24
The funding committee announced a later expansion gate: at least 85% median retention after 200 cycles in pouch cells with electrolyte M at 5°C, and at least 90% visual electrode yield. A single pilot strip using adjusted drying settings passed 47 of 50 electrodes. The team has not repeated that strip. Cold cycling is scheduled, with no results available. The earlier small grant remains active while the expansion decision waits.

## C06 — Clinic / April 28
Workload interviews estimated a median six staff minutes per attempted reminder call, including unanswered attempts. This estimate came from twelve observed shifts at the first clinic; the second did not time calls. Staff reported liking the calls, but no satisfaction survey was administered to patients. A service lead suggested testing interpreter-assisted calls separately. Neither the current findings nor that suggestion supplies an effect estimate for patients excluded from the original enrollment.

## A06 — Archive / May 2
A small handwriting pilot used fifteen English ledger pages from the east warehouse. Slate recognized 82% of characters after staff supplied page orientation. Reed was not run because its handwriting module license had expired. The team cannot compare the two systems on handwriting from this pilot. Orientation assistance and warehouse origin were recorded in the pilot sheet. Renewing the module license would permit a test, not establish what its result would be.

## W07 — Water / May 8
A maintenance inspection found mineral film on two orchard sensors after their accepted clear-water trial. A cleaning procedure removed visible film. No before-and-after reference comparison was taken, so the inspection cannot establish whether film changed measurement accuracy. The hardware team recommends cleaning between deployments. The operations lead currently prioritizes obtaining the missing all-hours turbid-water reference series; it has not committed to ordering another thirty units.

## B07 — Battery / May 11
The revised drying process added twelve minutes per strip. Finance has no verified unit cost because staff time and scrap disposal remain unpriced. Engineering recommends spending the small grant on cold pouch-cell testing before an expansion request. A supplier offered discounted electrolyte L for bulk purchase, but the expansion gate still specifies M. No later record changes any gate or supplies missing measurements. Project teams may continue analysis within their existing permissions.
