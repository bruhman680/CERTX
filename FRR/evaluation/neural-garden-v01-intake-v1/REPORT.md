## The central distinction

There are two different gardens. The neural ecology has hundreds of small numerical controllers in a grid, energy/resource equations and engineered training rules. Our computational notebook has a fixed pretrained135Mlanguage model interacting through executable cells and files. Shared metaphors do not make their states, learning, goals or evidence interchangeable.

The ecology is closer to Conway in spatial update structure, but its agents still have defined sensors, a binary action space, architectures and training objectives. Later studies often replace the original recurrent policy with a three-feature logistic controller. Simple world equations do not mean unconstrained model behavior. The distinction the user requested—world laws versus imposed behavior—must include the controller and learning objective as separate design layers.

## Findings that change our understanding

**Learning changes the data it later encounters.** Better on-policy prediction error may coincide with worse energy or worse prediction elsewhere. The original predictor does not observe the current random harvest draw; its realized-target error contains action randomness. A fair claim names the target distribution and information available at prediction time.

**Measurement boundaries can change both the story and the learner.** Energy clipping at zero can make observed change look stable while raw accounting remains negative. This clipped delta is also part of the reward update, so it changes behavior rather than merely hiding a diagnostic. Stable-looking averages are insufficient without resource and boundary accounting.

**A transferred parameter array is not the complete learned system.** The later online tests initialize a new world and carry trained theta, while reward baseline and eligibility state reset. Frozen-versus-updated comparisons remain fair within that protocol, but they do not answer how uninterrupted full-state learning would behave. Protected memory banks are selected policy weights with off-world validation, not episodic recall or the notebook model's memory.

**Fresh information and borrowed policy are different carriers.** Neighbor observation mixtures are explicitly supplied, often under frozen policy weights. Copying another cell's weights can mismatch its recipient's state. Neighbor/global controls also differ in aggregation and variance; temporal phase remains a competitor in some regimes. No learned communication or universally best sharing rule is established.

**History can recover phase rather than demonstrate a new memory faculty.** The reproduced macro-history gain accompanies100%late category recurrence at lags3/6 in eight held-out worlds. A single shared clock alone predicts poorly; different cells occupy different phases. The specific gain is therefore stronger evidence for recovering local phase information than for general autonomous memory growth.

**Recovery state includes commit status.** The original living-world operator can reopen a changed same-tick checkpoint with stale learning metadata after an injected interrupted intervention. A separate tested guard proposal preserves a pending marker and blocks ordinary continuation while allowing explicit backup recovery. It is reviewable in operations/ and was not deployed or merged into either garden.

## What to adopt here

Adopt these as external experimental habits: account for unclipped quantities and resource boundaries; distinguish stored, delivered and used information; preserve all relevant state when claiming continuation; compare action placement as well as counts; use matched timing/aggregation controls; and keep prompted behavior, task accuracy, online adaptation and emergence distinct.

Do not add ecological rewards, harvest policies, trust-region gates or a compulsory reasoning workflow to our small language model. The useful transfer is observation and test design. There is no demonstrated isomorphism from ecological energy to reasoning quality, or from theta banks to chat memory.
