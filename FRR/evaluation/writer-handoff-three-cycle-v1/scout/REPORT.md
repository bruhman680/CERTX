## What changes our experiment — our inference

Keep three outcomes separate:

1. **Fidelity:** does the handoff assert anything contradicted by its source or misbind a number, date, revision or uncertainty?
2. **Future-task sufficiency:** does it preserve what the prespecified later questions need, including relations rather than only isolated facts?
3. **Reader warrant:** does a reader ground its answer in available evidence, appropriately request originals, or report what remains unresolved?

A faithful handoff can omit information relevant to a changed task. More retained words do not automatically fix source authority, stale values or missing relationships. Conversely, an omitted detail irrelevant to every tested decision need not be a practical failure for that task family. These are experiment-design distinctions, not a theorem that arbitrary summaries preserve decisions.

Our strongest useful control is already in the planned contact: freeze future questions before writers act but keep them hidden from writers. Do not choose questions after seeing which facts a writer omitted. Preserve all actual handoffs even if one exceeds its word budget or contains an error; report that event without outcome retries. One writer per budget cannot identify a general causal effect of length. Later readers may get originals only under the declared access condition, with the same facts/rules otherwise.

The source offers no warranted new compression algorithm to install here. It sharpens the evaluator/target distinction and supports an explicit no-change decision to the frozen study. No task-specific training, MDP guarantee, numerical reward constant or graph-continuity rule transfers automatically.
