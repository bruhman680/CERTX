## Recurring inquiry

What survives a transformation, who can read it, and what later task does it support? This connects several branches as a question, not as proof of a universal physical mechanism. Forward movement asks what actions change; backward analysis asks which histories remain compatible; lateral comparison asks whether the same distinction matters in another substrate.

## What changed through correction

The noisy-echo simulation reused a random draw; corrected working copies preserve originals. RR1 retained its failed source prediction instead of retuning it away. A recovery evaluator's missing evidence belonged to the evaluation packet, not the actors. RR2's overly broad interim wording about zero prediction error was corrected to oracle-matched probabilities, with individual outcomes still random. The later annealing threshold/schedule arithmetic and reported population/group counts did not match their supplied narrative. These are concrete revisions to claims or implementations. They do not establish that FRR caused the repairs or is immune to similar mistakes. Preservation of correction history is useful evidence of the inquiry's movement, not proof that the whole archive is now verified.
