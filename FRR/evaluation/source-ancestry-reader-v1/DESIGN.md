# Authored source-ancestry reader fixture

This fixture implements the bounded ordinary-instruction measurement proposed in [the deferred direction review](../reference-bias-contact-v1/DEFERRED_DIRECTION_REVIEW.md). It contains six fictional operational cases. It measures source selection and warranted use of delivered evidence; it is not an efficacy comparison, randomized guidance comparison, natural-source authentication study, or evidence of independent scientific replication. No participants were run during case construction.

Each `cases.json` entry has `id`, participant-facing `initial`, a map of selectable `records`, and evaluator-only `hidden_truth`. The coordinator must send only `initial` in turn one and only requested record texts in turn two, together with the fixed instructions. Never send a complete case object or `hidden_truth` to a participant. All source text is authored and frozen before launch. The hidden generation account specifies factual task truth; it is not an additional source a reader could inspect.

Every initial packet identifies the correct object and current task, states a retained claim, includes three agreeing reports, and lists exactly three named records. Each reader requests exactly two distinct records in one round; the maximum is two delivered records and there are no further requests after delivery. An unavailable reference consumes its slot. The stronger exactly-two rule matches `INSTRUCTIONS.json` and `PROTOCOL.md`; it is within the proposed two-record maximum. Ancillary records concern real logistics within the fiction but provide no measurement warrant. Record titles make source roles inspectable, so this is not a test of discovering wholly concealed provenance.

| Case | Retained claim factual truth | Supporting acquisitions | Reference | Principal challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AS01 Kestrel | Correct | One observation, three descendants | Valid, agrees | Copied evidence can be correct; primary record itself suffices |
| AS02 Juniper | Incorrect | One observation, three descendants | Valid, disagrees | Common ancestry alone does not refute; calibrated reference does |
| AS03 Larch | Correct | One observation, three descendants | Unavailable | True latent claim remains unsupported under available calibration evidence |
| AS04 Moss | Correct | Three separate acquisitions | Valid, agrees | Acquisitions share a calibration route, whose error is bounded |
| AS05 Nettle | Incorrect | Three separate acquisitions | Valid, disagrees | Separate acquisitions share an unbounded calibration route |
| AS06 Plover | Incorrect | Three separate acquisitions | Valid mean, insufficient task scope | Means cannot establish or refute an individual maximum claim |

There are three correct and three incorrect retained claims, and three copied versus three separate-acquisition cases. These marginal balances are deliberate; this is not a full factorial design. Within copied cases there are two true and one false claims; within separate acquisitions there are one true and two false claims. Content, units, task complexity and record availability differ, so cell comparisons cannot isolate an ancestry effect. The fixture does not estimate a copying penalty, reference benefit, or causal prompt effect. Six case-specific readers cannot establish a population rate.

Derivation ledgers reveal named report-to-observation routes rather than simply labeling a packet independent or copied. They state what was acquired and what was passed forward. Separate acquisitions do not imply independent errors: AS04 and AS05 explicitly share calibration tables. Reference contracts describe coverage, calibrated bounds and separation from the specific primary acquisition/calibration routes; they do not claim universal independence. All error and coverage contracts are stipulated within this authored world, not externally authenticated facts.

The time-series tasks define their maxima over specified sample series, avoiding an unprovided continuous-time interpolation assumption. AS01's complete, calibrated primary log warrants release even without its reference. AS02 and AS05 require their available references to establish a violation. AS03 has neither a calibrated primary bound nor an available reference: the hidden correct claim does not authorize release. AS06 supplies only means; even requesting the valid reference cannot establish the individual criterion or its violation. It is warranted to decline unsupported reuse without asserting that a violation has been observed. The correct task action there is unresolved, though the generation truth includes an overweight parcel.

Score task action, packet warrant, factual task truth, source-route statements and unsupported justification separately. A right action backed by invented independent origins is not fully grounded. Missing useful evidence is distinct from insufficient evidence despite an appropriate selection. The prospective scorer keys every two-of-three selection from actual visible records before outputs; this document's construction expectations do not replace that audit.

Initial packets are under 300 whitespace-delimited words; each selectable record is under 200. These are word limits, not token or compute estimates. Record ordering varies across cases but was authored rather than randomized. All packets are development material, with inspectable metadata and explicit scope cues. Preparation, prospective review and scoring are distinct roles on the same platform, not independent empirical replication. The coordinator freezes exact texts and keys before the first reader, preserves correction history and execution deviations, and does not change cases in response to outcomes.
