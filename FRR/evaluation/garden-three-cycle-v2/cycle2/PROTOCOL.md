# Cycle2 adaptive decision experiment

Frozen before new execution, after inspecting Cycle1's exact ternary example with v in[.2,.8] and the peripheral scout's decision/identification distinction. This is authored decision analysis, not an empirical memory intervention or validation of a universal utility. No hidden-state point identification is assumed.

For a population with survival probability v, two actions have state losses: rely(S=0)=1,rely(S=1)=0; fallback(S=0)=.15,fallback(S=1)=.55. Thus expected losses are1-v and.15+.4v. These are fixed normative/task inputs, not learned costs. A mixed policy uses fallback probability q before seeing individual S. Compare pure-action dominance, minimax expected loss and minimax regret, allowing q in[0,1]. Intervals fixed as [.2,.8],[.75,.9],[.1,.3],[.55,.7]. Costs remain constant; don't choose costs to optimize observed empirical effects. No sampling: optimize piecewise-linear endpoint risks analytically by candidate intersections/endpoints; compare to a fixed10001-point grid for error bounds, not claim exact equality to discretization.

A probe observes each individual's post-intervention state S', not the population parameter v. With probability d, it destroys a surviving original S=1; S=0 stays0. Its additional loss is1 per original surviving record destroyed. After paying measurement cost k, choose the smaller state-specific action loss using perfect observation of S'. Expected total probe loss=k+.15*(1-(1-d)*v)+d*v. This assumes perfect readout, known damage probability, no other effects and correct loss specification. Cases(k,d)=(.03,0),(.5,0),(.03,.8). Compare worst expected losses over each interval to the optimized no-probe minimax policy. A naive calculation that ignores damage is a deliberately misspecified comparator. Learning v alone does not reveal individual S and is not this information experiment.

Predictions: some wide intervals admit the same dominant action without identifying v; crossing intervals can support different policies under worst loss versus regret; randomization can improve their expected criteria. Cheap state-preserving individual information can help under this contract, while costly/damaging information can lose its benefit. No claim that randomization guarantees a safe one-off realization or that a no-probe criterion certifies actual memory truth.

Checks: endpoint optimization vs direct loss evaluation; analytic optima within grid error<2e-4; dominance inequalities; explicit state/erase/probe enumeration agrees<1e-10; probabilities/mixtures admissible; source Cycle1 binding retained. At most4intervals×3probe variants plus criterion comparisons; no new stochastic draws, no threshold tuning, external models or fourth cycle. Preserve failures and repair separately if needed.
