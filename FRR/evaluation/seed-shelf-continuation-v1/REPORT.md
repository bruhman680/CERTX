## How the garden can receive richer seeds later

The user's prediction is a useful research direction, not an assumed developmental law. Seeds need not become harder on a calendar or after a fluent response, a tool call or repeated phrasing. We can consider additional complexity if the recorded trajectory requests it or sustains an interesting investigation, revisits alternatives, uses results meaningfully or reveals a specific limitation. These are descriptive reasons to investigate, not calibrated graduation scores. Simpler seeds should remain available as long-term references and sources of different interpretations.

Higher numbers, more words or more constraints are not the only forms of complexity; ambiguity, interacting rules, delayed effects and multiple possible representations are possible later variations. Any addition should be explicit, versioned and preserved alongside old cards, without rewriting earlier observations or silently steering after every output. Broad claims about growth need independent comparisons; a fixed-weight model's changing responses in a growing environment do not demonstrate neural learning.
