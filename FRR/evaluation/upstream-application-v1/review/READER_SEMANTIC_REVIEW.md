## Scorer limitations and useful consequence

Mechanical source checks are convenient for these saved cases but are not semantic judges: task A's generic substring '2' is weaker than explicit revision binding, and keywords alone can be copied into an unsupported answer. The manual review above confirms the actual replies have the needed relationships. Preserve the original scorer/results; do not promote it as a general evaluator without stronger source/semantic fixtures. Its assertion that all records were requested is an audit of observed behavior, not a generic handling policy for future readers who request none.

Visible revision/run/status cues and shared evidence-oriented guidance make these authored tasks relatively direct. Longer naturalistic context, omitted authority policy, contradictory/corrupted source data, writer-produced lossy records, denied lookup recovery and less-cued provenance remain untested. Whitespace word counts reflect JSON formatting as well as content; they are not token/latency/spending estimates.

The supported bridge is modest and useful: actual fresh readers used available current evidence for a conclusive decision, retained run identity, and left genuinely unreadable evidence unresolved. These outcomes are behavior under the given contract, not proof of hidden memory-carrier identification or a benefit caused by FRR/structured access. No current numerical implementation needed repair to explain them. Upstream tracing helped locate what to inspect: source revision, artifact identity and access status.
