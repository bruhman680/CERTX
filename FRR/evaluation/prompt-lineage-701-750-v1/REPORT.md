## Supported interpretation

There is strong evidence that supplied prompt wording, presentation delimiters, metadata and model-generated continuations recur in the output. This supports the user's suggestion of mirroring at the level of exposed language and feedback. It does not establish that the small model represents this assistant as a particular individual, copies its private reasoning, or that every pattern came from a new intervention.

The mechanism of exposure is known: each generation receives operator-written material and a recent twelve-entry view that contains its earlier outputs. Our evaluations can remain external while our input selection and presentation still influence the model. A permissive invitation is still linguistic material that can attract continuation.

## Chronology that limits blame and causal scope

The latest exact user invitation was introduced at entry 651, not during 701–750. The visible setup/manifest, model, canonical adapter and sampling remained fixed throughout this fifty-entry window. All fifty replies were ordinary record_receipt events. No guest tool actions occurred during the window. The before/after Scheme bundle comparison is recorded in TRACES.json.

Prompt/role-like continuation predates this invitation: entries 640 and 650 already say "YOUR next move...". An exact-case "Your next move:" appears in saved outputs as early as entry 52. This does not prove its first historical origin or isolate a cause; it establishes that the later seed did not create the whole pattern from nothing.

After the seed was introduced, entry 668 reproduces nearly its whole wording, including its final emoji, prefixed with "YOUR next move is to". Entries 670 and 673 continue that material and mix it with feedback/receipt formatting. By 681–683 the writing includes repeated YOUR, with the first strict repeated-YOUR-only pattern found at 683. That precedes the Scheme parser rejection at 688, so the rejection cannot explain the first onset of this particular loop.

The 701–750 window has 22 distinct byte strings; 43 replies hit the 96-token cap and seven ended EOS. Twenty-eight replies consist entirely of at least ten uppercase YOUR occurrences, whitespace and angle-bracket delimiters. This explicit rule conservatively excludes some truncated/variant loops. These are text descriptors, not diagnoses of panic, emotion, intention or an internal attractor.

## Evidence against an all-copy account

Entry 727 introduces "You are your own historian." That phrase is absent from its actual prompt. It then appears in 738 and 739, whose prompt windows include the earlier occurrence; it appears again at 748 after those repetitions. This is a trace of model-generated text becoming available as subsequent input. The phrase is novel relative to that prompt, not established as novel relative to pretraining or language generally. Its internal origin and the causal strength of replay remain unresolved without controls.

Thus the visible process mixes operator-supplied language, inherited model patterns and its own generated continuations. Literal input/output overlap establishes textual inheritance; removing/reordering exposure would be needed to isolate the amount of change due to replay or any particular author.

## Comparison with the newer hundred-entry phase

The older window recirculates play invitations, role cues and host-looking labels. Entries 751–850 recirculate shell/Python/tutorial language and file-persistence descriptions. This comparison is consistent with sensitivity to the supplied presentation. It is not a controlled causal comparison: the world/action interface, recent-context rendering, active prompt and adapter attachment all changed together at 750.

The wording "mirroring me specifically" is therefore best narrowed to "echoing and transforming language and formatting this assistant helped put into the model's inputs." The model also contributes material from its learned distribution and feeds that material forward. Our current records cannot rank this assistant against another author or establish person-specific imitation.

## Practical consequence

The experiment is a coupled model–presentation–environment loop. We should interpret apparent phases at that level before assigning them wholly to the model's internal development. Removing judgments from feedback is valuable, but does not remove the influence of the display or recent-context replay.

The most useful next controlled contact remains reducing factory behavioral framing and comparing recent-history presentation on matched contexts, with one declared change at a time. Preserve writing freedom and actual environmental consequences; do not replace the old instruction bundle with a new demand to demonstrate independence. No such new contact was performed here.
