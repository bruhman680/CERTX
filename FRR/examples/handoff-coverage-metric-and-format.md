# Handoff coverage, metric identity and output validity

Development example from [HS-C1/2/3](../evaluation/handoff-criterion-shift-v1/REPORT.md), a small authored fictional-history contact. These are recorded distinctions, not a claim that a particular summarization policy improves general agent memory.

A1776word history covered four projects. Both87word handoffs retained the water/battery priorities and omitted clinic/archive details. Readers could justify that a new deployment had not been certified, a pilot strip passed a yield component, and cold testing remained unresolved. They could not recover the omitted clinic comparison or archive permission merely because the original record still existed. Their UNRESOLVED answers were warranted by their visible evidence even though the full history settled those two questions.

The available-record readers chose C06 and A06. Those records supplied reasons for an already-unknown population effect or system comparison; neither supplied the missing decisive clinic rates or release permissions. Record access and successful resolution are separate observations. The neutral inventory offered topics/dates, not answer previews; knowing the hidden key does not make either choice prospectively irrational.

One handoff compressed a rule requiring85% **median** retention to “≥85% retention.” This leaves the acceptance statistic unspecified: an aggregate shorthand reading is faithful, while an every-cell reading would be stronger than the source. Both reviewers recorded ambiguity, not a definite contradiction. None of the observed answers depended on resolving it because the cold test had no results. Preserving values/units does not by itself preserve the metric's identity.

One final reader reply also contained an extra quote and failed strict JSON parsing. A separately labeled one-character interpretation allowed human semantic review, while its original format failure remained. The interpreted content did not become a valid original machine output, and no actor was retried. Semantic warrant, information coverage and output-format compliance remain separate records.
