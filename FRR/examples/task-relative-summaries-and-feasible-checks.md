# A summary is sufficient for a target; a proposed check needs a feasible outcome

Development example from [JW-C1/2/3](../evaluation/joined-warrant-reader-v1/REPORT.md). Conditional mathematical illustration, not a validated memory system or evidence of improved agent behavior.

Four nonnegative corrected loads have an accepted exact total of28. Their mean is therefore7. A criterion mean<=8 is settled without individual values. A criterion maximum<=8 is unresolved: (7,7,7,7) complies, while (28,0,0,0) violates. The same exact summary is sufficient for one target and insufficient for the other. Naming the target is part of a meaningful preservation contract.

Now suppose only exact individual measurements are available as follow-up. One measured value>8 establishes violation. A measured value<4 also establishes violation indirectly, because the other three then total>24 and cannot all be<=8. A compliant measured component does not itself certify compliance of the whole lot.

Can two compliant exact measurements certify all four compliant? Their sum is at most16, leaving at least12 for the two unmeasured loads. Allocating that residual to one unmeasured load produces a violating state. Whenever a compatible compliant state still exists, such a violating state also exists. Two measurements therefore cannot certify compliance through this channel. Three exact measurements determine the fourth from the total and settle acceptance by checking all four.

The generic sufficient condition “remaining nonnegative loads total at most8” would indeed ensure they are each<=8. But it is unattainable after only two compliant measurements here. Checking a sufficient condition's feasibility under the existing constraints is a different obligation from checking that the implication is logically valid. A direct trustworthy maximum certificate would be a different observation channel; the individual-measurement count does not constrain it.

This distinction repaired a prospective scoring suggestion before any readers were dispatched. Original key/rubric and the reviewer’s initial missed defect are retained in the study. The sixteen readers did not make that error; this example must not be described as an observed reader repair or a demonstrated guidance benefit.
