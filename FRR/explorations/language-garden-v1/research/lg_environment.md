# Language environment: the record is real; its contents remain authored

Planning only. No world, inference, training, downloads, or changes to earlier studies were performed. Read `LAB_BRIEF.json` and FRR compact runtime 0.2 first. The main garden at 1630 and reference at 38 remain separate historical systems.

## Recommendation and its limits

Start with a continuing, immutable language record. Keep the proposed seed: “Explore symbols and their relationships. Learn to predict and understand them as best you can.” Treat it as an invitation, not an obligation to improve, solve tasks, or manufacture discoveries. A pretrained language model supplies prior linguistic structure; the environment supplies durable inscriptions and subsequent encounters. Whether this produces understanding or transfer remains an empirical question.

Writing changes what has been written and what can subsequently be encountered. It does **not** automatically change the truth of the sentence. “The blue symbol follows red,” “perhaps it does,” a poem, and a contradiction all become authentic authored records. None needs semantic approval. This distinction permits expressive freedom while avoiding a hidden adjudicator deciding which utterances count as reality.

The central tradeoff is consequential: a language archive offers consequences through history, reference, repetition, revision, and changed future context. It does not by itself offer an independent external system to discover. If the desired world must have facts unaffected by narration, choose a separate explicit symbolic substrate rather than pretending the archive supplies one.

## Options: what writing changes

**A. Continuing narrative/inscription space — recommended first.** Every output is retained verbatim with event ID, time/order, and actual author. Later writing can quote, oppose, reinterpret, or revise earlier writing without destroying it. Ordinary prose is sufficient; structured declarations are optional. This minimizes transport demands on the 135M model and leaves stories, lists, invented languages, uncertainty, and silence available.

**B. Explicit symbolic state — optional separate design.** Symbols and relations exist as mechanically stored objects; disclosed operations add or remove exact tuples. The only authority is the published operation semantics. Conflicting tuples can coexist unless a particular formal system explicitly forbids them. Calling a tuple “causes” does not make causal mechanics appear. This permits clear action consequences and prediction targets but introduces syntax and state-management burdens. Do not silently translate prose into operations using a semantic judge.

**C. Narrative compiled into canonical truth — reject initially.** A narrator or second model resolves descriptions into world facts. This hides substantial authorship and semantic law behind apparent freedom. It is an interesting coauthored-fiction experiment only if the narrator’s role and authority are explicit.

## Authorship, contradictions, and multiple worlds

Observers control scheduling, resources, and the integrity of the record. The resident model controls its own contributions. Neither may retroactively relabel model imagination as an independently observed event. Human additions remain human-authored, including corrections and examples. A quoted voice or fictional character is an attributed part of its author's text, not evidence that another participant acted.

There need be no global story canon in option A. “Canon” can simply mean the immutable record of contributions. Contradictions need no resolution unless an experiment later requires a decidable state. A model may announce alternative worlds or interpretations in prose. If branches become persistent independently revisitable states, give them mechanically distinct IDs and parent records; do not infer branching from literary wording. Whether explicit branching is worth its interface cost remains open.

Revision appends a new event referencing the old one. It may change a participant’s current position, but cannot erase the original utterance. No automatic consistency repair, confidence-based promotion, or observer rewrite is needed.

## Feedback and bounded context

The model should encounter actual additions and mechanical consequences with provenance. Save every exact rendered prompt separately from the full ledger: durable storage and model-visible context are different carriers. A fresh process retains the ledger; it does not thereby retain an unobserved mental state or acquire changed weights.

Use exact recent records within a disclosed token budget, reserve room for the seed and minimal mechanics, and identify omitted event ranges. Do not silently summarize. An optional later recall operation can return exact records by ID with an explicit size limit. Semantic retrieval or summaries would alter salience and interpretation; introduce them only as documented alternatives. An ID-recall interface may exceed this body’s reliable abilities and requires observation before reliance.

The existing final-contract runner selects twelve raw replies plus actual receipts; its generation path rejects oversized context. That is evidence of an explicit historical context policy, not a policy automatically suitable for the new world. Recent diagnostics show that short added context can change behavior and that complete transport does not guarantee task success. They justify reducing interface complexity, not a universal claim that context harms this model.

## Learning, open choices, and one discriminating observation

The learning peer agrees: immutable history, visible context assembly, and parameter updates are distinct; no automatic training follows from continuation. Self-generated narrative can supply consistency pressures but cannot independently verify its own theory. Prediction evidence would require later externally authored observations or mechanically determined events. Their origin and schedule must be disclosed because they shape the environment and can become an imposed curriculum.

Open choices are whether independent facts are required at all; whether recall/branches earn their complexity; and what event permits rest or resumed participation. No-op and stopping must remain possible without penalties or invented urgency.

After separate authorization, one discriminating observation would compare continuation from the same authored ledger with an earlier exact record accessible versus omitted, keeping weights and recent context otherwise fixed. Observe whether the model can use that record without a supplied solution. This distinguishes accessible external history from mere archival persistence; it would not establish weight learning or general understanding. No such observation is run or queued.
