# Training-domain audit: SmolLM2-135M-Instruct

Planning only. Session date: 20 November 2026, America/Chicago; original contributor host date: 21 November 2026 UTC. Read `LAB_BRIEF.json` and the full `compact.md`; neither inference nor training was performed. Claims below concern published provenance, not a new behavioral result or verification of cached weights against their recorded revision.

## What survives verification

**Author result.** The model authors' paper §6 explicitly assigns **2T pretraining tokens to 135M**, 4T to 360M, and a single-stage high-quality mixture to these smaller models. Stack-Edu, InfiMM-WebMath, FineMath and Cosmopedia enter from the start; DCLM is filtered using the FineWeb-Edu classifier. The roughly11T, four-stage schedule dominating the paper belongs to **1.7B**. Its ablations and strong chat/math results cannot silently become135M evidence. The135M card independently states2T, although card and paper share authorship and are not independent replication.

**Stages differ.** Pretraining teaches text continuation using the corpus mixture. Small-model SFT then uses filtered SmolTalk: the released small dataset card says shorter Smol-Magpie-Ultra conversations, less rewriting/summarization, no function calling, and no advanced-math datasets. UltraFeedback DPO follows. Those exclusions concern SFT; they do not mean absence of mathematical or code material from base pretraining.

The published small recipes confirm this sequence. Their model paths default to360M with comments saying the scripts are also used for135M. SFT specifies two epochs and8192 maximum sequence length; DPO specifies two epochs,1024 maximum length and512 maximum prompt length. These are recipe settings, not executed135M training logs. In particular,8192 is a sequence ceiling, not evidence of uniformly long conversations. The SFT template inserts a helpful-assistant system message when no system message is present. Assistant-style continuation is therefore a serious rival explanation for apparently purposeful prose.

**Tokenizer distinction.** Paper §4.1 describes a49,152-vocabulary tokenizer trained on70% FineWeb-Edu,15% Cosmopedia-v2,8% OpenWebMath,5% StarCoderData and2% StackOverflow. That is tokenizer-construction provenance, not the LM's pretraining mixture. The paragraph describes the main1.7B setup; I did not independently establish the cached135M tokenizer's exact training provenance. The GPT-2 tokenizer mentioned in §3.1 belongs to dataset ablations, another distinct setup.

## Proportions and counting limits

The prior EleutherAI scout is broadly accurate **as a curator-derived approximation**. Its sample README lists34B FineMath,125B Stack-Edu,40B InfiMM-WebMath,30B Cosmopedia-v2,710.4B FineWeb-Edu and1065.6B DCLM-Edu. These sum to **2.005T**, yielding88.58% web,6.23% code,3.69% math and1.50% Cosmopedia under those categories. These are arithmetic on the curator's listed source quantities, not measured shares of the10B sample or authoritative consumed-token logs. The curator's DCLM/FineWeb6:4 ratio also must not be replaced with the main paper's earlier FineWeb/DCLM6:4 ratio: ordering and model scope differ. Author §6 supports source membership and total scale, but does not supply that exact per-source ledger. No authoritative135M consumed-token log was located in this bounded audit; exact proportions remain unresolved.

The current small-SFT card reports **460,341 train rows** and24,229 test rows. These are current released-dataset rows, not verified historical checkpoint exposure. The prior58.8% short-Magpie and10.4% self-oss-instruct figures were not independently recounted here: the card lacks those counts, and dataset downloads were excluded. Even correct source-row proportions would not establish token shares, effective loss contribution, epochs actually consumed, or checkpoint-specific exposure. Recipes and current dataset revisions can differ from historical training.

## What this means for the garden

**Supported scope:** English text and short instruction/conversation exposure make plain English a reasonable available interface. The official135M card describes primarily English generation and warns about factual/logical inconsistency. Its own evaluations report IFEval29.9 and GSM8K1.4; these are task-specific card scores, not probabilities of garden success. No read source directly evaluates persistent voluntary exploration, creative symbol invention, stable self-chosen relationships, recovery after interruption, or transfer to other systems. Genre exposure is not demonstrated competence at those activities.

**Design proposal:** keep plain English available alongside arbitrary symbols and optional code; do not make executable code the only entry route. This is an accessibility hypothesis, not a ranking of language versus code viability. Keep any observer labels, participation counts and provenance outside the model-facing seed. Do not impose conversational turns or reward semantic approval merely because SFT used an assistant format.

**Cannot establish:** training provenance cannot establish that a recurring relation expresses newly acquired understanding, rather than familiar continuation, prompt uptake or context-carried reconstruction. A discriminating future observation, only after separate run authorization, would compare retention/use of a newly chosen relation under a controlled contextual change against an otherwise matched continuation without its history; continued participation and relation use should be recorded separately. No such test ran here.

Cross-review with `lg_challenge` accepted the narrow accessibility implication and rejected a viability ranking. Its consequential rival is that fluent English invites tutorial/assistant completion instead of chosen exploration. We agree this remains open; peer review shares inputs and is not independent replication.
