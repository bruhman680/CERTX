# SIDE expansion: repair is an affordance, not an acceptance gate

**Authorization and scope.** Read round1/DECISIONS.json. Root authorized one adjacent primary-source contact after inspecting the initial mechanism and critical review. This document uses that one contact; no further widening, implementation, model runs or training occurred. Initial files remain unchanged. This is a justified research expansion, not evidence that the scouting method or proposed model arrangement works.

## Actual source contact

Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs (1986), *Referring as a collaborative process*, Cognition 22, 1–39. [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1980s/Clark,%20H.H.%20_%20Wilkes-Gibbs,%20D.%20_Referring%20as%20a%20collaborative%20process_%201986.pdf). Read theoretical examples/model and the experiment's method, quantitative results, transcript analysis and relevant discussion, especially pp.11–35; references not audited. PDF text was inspected without saving a source artifact.

**Evidence:** eight pairs of Stanford undergraduates arranged twelve unfamiliar tangram figures across six trials. Director and matcher could converse but not see each other's arrays. Participants knew their figure sets were identical, had a common arrangement goal, were timed/tape-recorded, and received positions of mismatches after trials. The reported placement error rate was 2%. Directors' mean words per figure decreased from 41 to 8; turns decreased from 3.7 to roughly one. Transcript analyses document requests for expansion, offered alternative descriptions, repair and confirmation. Descriptions became shorter as mutually used perspectives developed.

**Limits:** this is not a randomized comparison of repair versus no repair. Repetition, familiarization, shared purpose, feedback and existing human competence all contribute. The sample/task does not establish that optional repair alone creates shared understanding, or that fixed-weight small models can do likewise. The authors distinguish mutually believed understanding from actual understanding; acknowledgment can be mistaken or insincere. Their discussion explicitly notes that the high accuracy goal encouraged explicit acceptance.

## Functional bridge and changed design choice

**Proposal:** change the initial one-message exchange into an available reciprocal channel. After an initial description, the other context may ask for clarification, propose an interpretation, modify an artifact, leave it unresolved, or disengage. The original context may respond. No prescribed linguistic repair sequence, mandatory question, reward, mutual-acceptance condition or correctness gate enters the garden. All contributions remain recorded regardless of success.

The bridge is functional: receiver responses can expose which distinction a description failed to communicate, allowing subsequent messages to address that distinction. It does not import human capacities, the source's timed goal, or its measured compression into this body. Observer annotations may identify attempted clarification; words like “yes” alone cannot certify a common referent. Brevity can reflect an efficient convention, copying, or abandoned effort.

## Root's countercondition: reference must be identifiable

The initial graph contact was underspecified. If two nodes are interchangeable under every graph automorphism preserving the shared observable evidence, independently permuted local names provide no basis for recovering which node corresponds to a sender's private target. More conversation about the same invariant structure cannot distinguish them. Negotiating an arbitrary new convention is different from recovering that preexisting correspondence.

**Revision:** before interpreting failures, establish that the target is uniquely distinguishable from the available shared structure; otherwise evaluate an identifiable orbit/set, or provide a target-independent shared anchor or jointly observed action that breaks the relevant symmetry. One anchor need not eliminate every symmetry. Keep any anchor equally available across controls and verify it does not reveal the chosen target. Additional dialogue can resolve an insufficient description, but cannot manufacture missing identifying information.

## One revised falsifiable contact

In a detached optional encounter with identifiable targets, compare reciprocal exchange against one-way description under equal total message-token budgets and equal receiver evidence. Account separately for host calls and context. Retain distribution-matched shuffled/no-message controls to challenge ID/order leakage and pretrained inference without communication. Do not force an action after clarification.

**Discriminant:** a clarification that addresses an actual ambiguity is followed by a changed selection consistent with the newly supplied distinction; matched one-way/shuffled messages do not produce the same change. This would support useful repair in that contact. Generic agreement, equivalent outcomes without informative messages, or unidentifiable targets defeat the stronger interpretation. Unequal participation can leave the comparison inconclusive. The strongest revised implication is to permit repair while first proving the task supplies enough information for repair to matter.
