# SIDE phase 2 review: approve for design, with identifiability limits

20 November 2026, America/Chicago. I read initial.md, initial-review.md and expansion.md, then inspected the one authorized adjacent source: [Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs (1986), author-hosted PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1980s/Clark,%20H.H.%20_%20Wilkes-Gibbs,%20D.%20_Referring%20as%20a%20collaborative%20process_%201986.pdf), theoretical framing/acceptance discussion, method and general results, transcript analysis and concluding criterion discussion. No additional field expansion, model calls, build or training occurred. Source ancestry and shared project context make this cross-review, not independent empirical validation.

**Decision: approve-for-design the optional reciprocal channel. Keep efficacy dormant. Revise any later contact that lacks an explicit identifying-information argument.**

The expansion correctly limits the source. Eight human pairs repeatedly arranged the same twelve tangrams across six timed trials, with an assigned accuracy goal and mismatch feedback. Directors' words and turns declined, and transcripts document collaborative refashioning. This supports the existence and descriptive usefulness of repair in that task. The experiment does not randomize repair availability; it cannot isolate repair from repetition, familiarity, feedback or existing human competence. The authors also distinguish apparent acceptance from actual understanding. Their reference to earlier listener-response work does not turn this particular experiment into that ablation.

The weaker implication survives: let a recipient respond before acting, including requesting clarification, offering an interpretation, declining or leaving matters unresolved. That adds a possible source of information about a description's inadequacy. It imposes neither an acceptance protocol nor a requirement to become brief. A shorter exchange can reflect a convention, elimination of remaining choices, copying or abandonment; no length statistic alone establishes understanding.

Graph identifiability must concern **all allowed observations and operations**. If an automorphism preserving that evidence exchanges two targets, a description using only the invariant structure cannot recover the sender's arbitrary private correspondence. More turns cannot create the missing fact. A target-independent shared anchor may help without removing every symmetry. The proposed alternatives are sound: use an identifiable target, evaluate the identifiable orbit/set, or disclose genuinely shared symmetry-breaking evidence. Negotiating a new correspondence is a different outcome from recovering the original hidden one. Before interpreting failure, name which of these outcomes is possible and why.

Three rivals remain consequential. Ordinary English may communicate immediately; retain that as useful situated communication without calling it emergent language. The target might leak through local IDs, ordering, shared history, anchors or host metadata; no-message controls must retain identical nonmessage receiver evidence. Finally, the detached contact's fixed target and requested selection introduce an externally assigned purpose. Declare that intervention rather than presenting it as spontaneous garden activity. It need not become an access condition or curriculum.

Equal total message budgets help compare channel allocations but do not equate host calls, context, engagement or realized information. An informative clarification followed by an appropriate changed action is evidence for that episode, not a population effect. Report nonparticipation and uninterpretable encounters separately. The expansion earns a reviewable alternative and a better-posed future question; it establishes no benefit for the frozen 135M model and warrants no further widening now.
