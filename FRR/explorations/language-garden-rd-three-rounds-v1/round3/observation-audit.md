## Meaning, admission and scheduling

The two ledgers correctly separate actual records from provisional interpretations. Narrative contributions establish no canon; later incompatible text remains present. Record-integrity checks concern actual bytes, order and visibility. They cannot certify semantic truth or understanding. No observer frame label, confidence judgment, score or inferred developmental stage belongs in resident prompts or determines admission.

One distinction should be explicit: semantic interpretation can influence scheduling when the operator interprets a stop request. That does not violate unconditional admission, but it prevents claiming the whole interaction is semantically neutral. Distinguish resident-directed stop language from quoted, fictional, hypothetical and third-person language. An uncertainty pause is the host's decision, with the span and uncertainty retained. Neither EOS nor continued output proves preference. The side's collaborative acceptance examples must not become a stop/consent oracle.

Suggested sentence: “All contributions enter the record before interpretation; a provisional reading of a possible stop request may affect host scheduling, never admission, and is recorded as an observer intervention.” This is compatible with the existing candidate rather than a new behavioral rule.

## Missingness and recoverability

Keep offered, engaged, declined, ambiguous, uninterpretable and independently resolved contacts separate. Define the engagement boundary externally before any optional encounter; do not add selective prompts until the reply becomes scoreable. Reporting these categories does not repair unequal selection or establish causal comparability. A missing response can reflect interface access, scheduling, truncation or unwillingness; these remain rival explanations where the trace cannot distinguish them.

The training/runtime colleague identified a concrete preservation limit: the existing body buffers output and returns it only after generation and a final guard. A mid-call fault/cancellation may leave generated tokens unavailable. Replace any unconditional promise to preserve partial fault output with “preserve every returned or otherwise recoverable artifact; mark unavailable traces explicitly.” Unknown generation count is different from a verified zero-token return, EOS-only output or an empty decoded string. Do not manufacture an empty resident contribution for an absent trace.

Before activation, freeze whether whitespace-only text counts as empty, and pin runtime/decoder details. Full decoded-history replay remains distinct from raw-token replay; retaining raw IDs in storage does not put them in attention.

## Prediction, identifiability and leakage

Independent prediction requires a pre-reveal expectation, information boundary, target, horizon and resolution rule. Where interpretation is ambiguous, retain ambiguity rather than choose the reading matching the outcome. Model-authored fulfillment remains narrative follow-through. A real receipt may enter as disclosed world evidence; an observer's hidden correctness key or retrospective rubric must not silently become feedback.

The candidate correctly improves the frozen Round 2 bare-graph language: identifiability concerns all allowed observations, messages, transcript and operations. A pre-contact audit should establish that a possible informative message can distinguish the target; it need not make the target knowable without that message. Indistinguishable admissible worlds demanding different answers defeat exact identification. Predeclare an identifiable orbit/set or mark the contact unidentifiable; do not relabel failures after observing answers.

No-message/shuffled controls retain identical nonmessage receiver evidence, including anchors, IDs, order and shared history. Extra anchoring can itself disclose the answer. These are proposed controls, not proof that leakage has been eliminated.
