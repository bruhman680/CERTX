# Optional practice: a three-angle source scout

Use when a consequential uncertainty would benefit from outside knowledge. The user proposed making our successive sweeps a reusable habit. This saved procedure is an adopted optional aid, not a demonstrated optimal research method or a requirement for every conversation.

Start with a decision: what could this scout change in the next experiment, build or interpretation? Set a small source/time budget and inspect existing records before duplicating searches. No extra form is necessary.

| Angle | Operation | Useful return |
|---|---|---|
|Direct|Find the closest source, method, reproduction or prior contact.|What already works, and under which conditions?|
|Peripheral|Search another field sharing the relevant operator, measurement problem or decision.|One transferable control, metric or rival; matching vocabulary is insufficient.|
|Challenge / underlying|Try to break the proposed transfer: information access, selection, calibration, confounding, cost or missing cases.|A discriminating observation, narrower claim, or justified unresolved question.|

These are angles, not mandatory sequential stages. Skip or combine them when useful. One decisive source can end a scout; completing three passes is not itself progress. Existing read sources can supply an angle without another search.

## Source and claim discipline

- Save primary title/version/date/URL and actual read depth. Search snippets and screenshots are leads. Different versions or shared data/authorship do not automatically supply independent evidence.
- Before changing a design, inspect the relevant method/result and assumptions/limitations, or explicitly mark missing access. Keep the paper's result, our interpretation and our proposed transfer distinct.
- Keep a rival or countercondition visible. Where feasible give a challenger the claim/artifacts without the preferred conclusion; record prior exposure. Role separation is not proof of independence.
- For “training-free” or capability claims, trace all information and work: pretrained weights, generated prompts, programs, external records/corpora, adapters/readouts, stronger builders, validation feedback and inference compute. No gradient update does not mean no information or no cost.
- Report a remembered source as unidentified until enough identifying evidence is available. Preserve plausible candidates without silently choosing one.

## Upstream dependency trace, when consequential

The underlying/challenge angle includes implementation and data-generation dynamics. Before explaining an apparent downstream effect, ask where the distinction it depends on first enters, changes or disappears. A candidate trace is source generation/access → representation (units, tokenization, dtype, clipping) → numerical operations → observation/calibration → objective/evaluator → inference → action/feedback. This is a working dependency sketch; stages can be coupled, reordered or absent, and actions can change earlier conditions.

For the next consequential decision, select the earliest plausible failure boundary and a discriminating check: preserved inputs, alternate precision, an invariant, known stationary case, original-source access or evaluator calibration. Compare intended mathematics with computation on the actual stored inputs. A stable downstream formula cannot recover distinctions already lost at input. Passing an invariant does not by itself establish numerical or scientific correctness.

Use direct and peripheral sources to challenge that boundary, keeping each substrate's mechanism separate. Do not explain every error through a newly found paper, recursively audit everything, or make this a fourth mandatory scouting angle. Stop when the next action is warranted. [UN-C1](../evaluation/upstream-numerics-v1/REPORT.md) demonstrates the numerical/representation distinction with explicit formulas, not training reproduction.

## Stop and use

Stop when the budget is reached, or when one justified change to the next decision/test is clear and remaining uncertainty is named. If more searching would not change that choice, return to testing. “No change warranted” is a valid outcome; do not manufacture a new queue family for every paper or start an automatic fourth sweep.

Record the smallest useful return: source/condition, proposed transfer, rival or falsifier, next action, and what remains untested. After practical use, note whether the scout changed a control, measurement or action. Saved instructions and successful source retrieval do not prove a durable behavior improvement.
