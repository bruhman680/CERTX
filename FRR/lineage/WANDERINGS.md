## Prospective witnesses and retrospective reading

User seed, 5 October 2026: “enable future agents (before wave propagation) to observe ... a backward future/past observation angle of memory continuity or movement.” Context: wake inheritance, actor reset/substitution, and write–retain–read interfaces.

Two readings remain active:

1. Place observers now at locations or stages that the wave will reach later. They can record their local field before arrival, during passage, and afterward. They are future-relative recipients, not observers accessing a future event before it occurs.
2. Later agents read retained traces and infer compatible earlier changes. This is retrospective inference; several histories can yield the same trace unless additional observations distinguish them.

A useful candidate is an observation handoff: an early witness records local conditions, passage leaves a mark, and a later or reset reader compares that mark with the retained baseline. This can connect episodic actors through an environmental carrier without requiring one actor's continuous internal memory.

Distinguish direct future observation from a prediction or simulation prepared now. Distinguish backward interpretation from backward physical propagation. The observation geometry can span past and future-relative stages while causal interactions proceed forward in time.

Candidate comparisons: no observer; passive logger; active measuring observer; reset observer retaining its external log; fresh observer receiving only the field; fresh observer receiving field plus baseline. Logs and field changes are separate carriers. Active probes may alter propagation or create additional wakes.

Open question: is continuity carried by the field, the witness record, a reconstruction rule, or their combination? A successful reading at successive stations does not prove the original distinction travelled unchanged; receivers may transform or regenerate it. Compare preservation of a specific distinction, not merely agreement of descriptions.

## Substrate continuity and prospective stations

User steering: continuity may belong to the substrate itself, and parallel exploration should consider where past/future-relative observations would be less compromised.

Three parallel passes examined substrate mechanisms, observer placement, and a toy witness construction. Shared source framing and model context make their output a synthesis, not independent evidence. Useful alternatives: retained deformation, travelling modes, regenerated patterns, and persistent accessibility. They can coexist; no universal mechanism is inferred.

A prospective station can record conditions before arrival, during passage, and afterward. A fresh reader introduced afterward can receive only the substrate, only authentic earlier records, or both. Independently prepared no-wave controls and deliberately mismatched records can expose expectation and record leakage. Earlier observations must actually be recorded earlier; retrospective reconstruction is different.

Observer placement has two obligations: limit causal disturbance and limit interpretive leakage. Remote/upstream location alone does not provide isolation where reflections, nonlocal coupling, or shared controllers exist. A copied simulation snapshot can define a noninteracting read within the model. Real probes require disturbance comparisons, stated resolution, calibration, and causal boundaries. Hashes authenticate unchanged bytes, not correct measurement.

`experiments/substrate_witness_probe.py` implements a deliberately constructed pulse and retained trail on a finite line. Opposite histories start with the same pulse mass and produce equal retained mass but different spatial trails after the pulse exits. The distinction survives a field-only fresh-reader interface while a total-mass summary erases it. No-write, fast-erasure, and no-pulse controls remove the retained signal. Active extraction changes later fields; copied reads do not. This demonstrates specified model behavior, not a discovered general mechanism or blind reader performance.

Candidate criterion: an earlier distinction is carried by the substrate when fresh readers can recover its consequences without inherited observer logs, and appropriate substrate interventions remove or alter that recovery. A controlled learned-reader evaluation must separate calibration from evaluation and include held-out inscriptions. Keep the possibilities of transport, retention, and renewal open until contact distinguishes them.

Backward ambiguity check: a nonzero initial field prepared as m_0=m_T/rho^T reproduces the same final trail by decay alone with no pulse. The toy verifies this numerically. Without a known or measured baseline, a future-only reader cannot identify which of those histories occurred. Continuity of an observable does not guarantee unique historical reconstruction.
