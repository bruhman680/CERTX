## Constructive and rival readings

A boundary could protect a fragile distinction from destructive averaging. It could also retain a misconception by blocking correction. Different regions remaining different does not distinguish these outcomes. A boundary may carry historical influence in communication weights or available edges without preserving a readable message. Conversely, shared substrate access can bypass an apparent graph boundary. The operational graph must include actual reads and writes, not only messages.

Four carriers deserve separate interventions: actor policies, communication topology, stored substrate values, and the orchestrator's scheduler/budgets. Retaining the graph while replacing actors tests inherited accessibility; retaining field values while reconnecting regions tests isolation; shuffling values while preserving topology tests encoded content; replacing the scheduler tests imposed opportunity limits. Physical or implementation feasibility sets the scope of each swap.

## Opened source contact

**Holme & Newman (2006), Nonequilibrium phase transition in the coevolution of networks and opinions.** https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0603023 ; https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.74.056108 . Abstract and PDF opened. Opinion adoption and preferential linking are combined, producing a model transition between diverse opinions and extensive agreement. This provides a concrete genealogy for coevolving boundaries, not validation of a reasoning swarm. Its states are opinions rather than warranted beliefs; agreement does not establish truth.

**Vazquez, Eguiluz & San Miguel (2008), Generic Absorbing Transition in Coevolution Dynamics.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.4910 ; https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.108702 . Full PDF opened. Binary voter states on initially degree-regular random graphs; disagreeing links either rewire toward same-state nodes or trigger adoption. Finite systems eventually absorb. For mean degree four, their mean-field threshold is 2/3, while simulations give approximately 0.38; the paper attributes the discrepancy to rewiring correlations. Thus use the mechanism and compare measured thresholds, rather than borrowing an exact boundary formula. No spontaneous innovations or evidence-based adjudication are built into this model.

**Gross & Blasius (2008), Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2405905/ . Located as a genealogical bridge; opening returned a browser challenge. Not audited in this scout and not counted as additional confirmation.

These papers share a research neighborhood; their relationship is genealogy, not independent empirical confirmation of FRR. Search aperture: coevolving opinions and topology, absorbing fragmentation, competition of interaction/adoption timescales. Unsearched repairs include innovation, random reconnection, evidence-sensitive linking, and migration; each needs its own source contact before stronger claims.

## Strongest next contact

After a candidate boundary forms, introduce a randomized, held-out correction or task-relevant distinction into one region. Measure both local retention and transfer to the other region. Then restore a small communication bridge while holding field values and actor rules fixed. Repeat with fresh readers, erased/shuffled substrate, and matched random or static topology.

- Retention plus failed transfer suggests isolation, not accessible shared memory.
- Restored transfer after bridging locates an accessibility limitation.
- Fresh readers recovering the randomized distinction supports substrate carriage for that readout task.
- A correction being rejected despite an available route suggests policy/evaluation resistance; topology alone is insufficient.

Measure correct updating as well as agreement, and retain deliberately incorrect inscriptions as rivals. A boundary's usefulness depends on what it preserves, what it admits, and which failures it allows correction of. Shared agent output is synthesis, not independent evidential convergence.

## Provisional crystallization

**A boundary is selective permeability. Its value is not the persistence of difference alone, but whether consequential distinctions remain both recoverable and revisable under declared access and intervention conditions.** Keep this as an operational candidate. Walls may emerge from adaptive interaction; permanence, semantic memory, and improvement require separate tests.
