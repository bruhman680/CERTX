## Part A: how long must a seed be before "a rule fits it" means something?
Exact count of distinct length-n sequences generated by a rule family, divided by the number of possible sequences (a random sequence is "explained" with that probability).
| family | n=2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | smallest n with false-rule prob < .05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| affine mod m, m<=30, values 0-99 | .090 | .025 | 1.2e-3 | 1.3e-5 | 1.4e-7 | 3 |
| affine mod m, m<=60, values 0-99 | .360 | .207 | .0195 | 2.2e-4 | 2.2e-6 | 4 |
| affine mod m, m<=10, digits 0-9 | 1.000 | .810 | .148 | .0156 | .0016 | 5 |
- The false-rule probability falls geometrically with n and rises with family size and with a small alphabet. With digits, any 2-number seed fits some rule in the family, and a 4-digit seed still fits one with probability about .15.
- Predictions: A1 partly FAILED (n=3 for the m<=30 family was .025, below my guessed .05-.25; n=4 and n=5 bounds held); A2 FAILED (m<=60, n=3 was .207, I predicted >= .40, because many generated sequences coincide); A3 missed narrowly (digits n=4 was .148 vs predicted .15-.40; n=6 held); A4 (the needed n grows with family size) held: 3, 4, 5.
- Design reading: a seed's length should be chosen against the family of rules the verifier will accept; the larger that family or the smaller the alphabet, the longer the seed must be before a "found pattern" has any evidential weight. A claim-scorer should discount a found rule by its family size, in the Occam sense.

## Part B: can the planned measures tell agent types apart? (n=8 seeds, 400 per cell)
Copy flag / rule-consistency (match to the true generator's next 8 values):
| agent | SQUARES | FIB | PARITY | CONST | SHUFFLE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| COPY | 1.00/0.00 | 1.00/0.00 | 1.00/1.00 | 1.00/1.00 | 1.00/- |
| FLUENT (bigram) | .01/.01 | .02/.01 | 1.00/1.00 | 1.00/1.00 | .03/- |
| RULE | .00/1.00 | .00/1.00 | 1.00/1.00 | 1.00/1.00 | .02/- |
| RANDOM | .00/.01 | .00/.01 | .00/.01 | .00/.01 | .00/- |
RULE agent false-rule rate on SHUFFLE seeds: n=4 .0097, n=5 .0002, n>=6 0.0000 (20000 seeds each).
- B1 held for SQUARES/FIB (copy flag 1.00, rule-consistency 0.00) but missed in detail: a copier also scores rule-consistency 1.00 on PARITY, not only CONST. B2 held. B3 held. B4 held.
- **Main finding:** on periodic seeds (CONST, PARITY) a copier, a local-fluency continuer and a rule-finder are indistinguishable by copy flag and rule-consistency. Only non-local structure (squares, Fibonacci) separates fluency from rule-finding, and a transfer test would separate copying from rule use.
- Design reading: (i) do not use periodic seeds alone to say a model found a rule; (ii) include non-local generators; (iii) plant a later seed from the same generator with a different starting point, which a pure copier cannot continue (observed post hoc, no instruction required); (iv) keep a matched shuffled seed so false-rule claims are visible.

## Limits
Toy agents and my own hypothesis family; the false-rule numbers describe those families only, not a language model's pareidolia; seeds use values 0-99 and digits, not tokens; shows what the planned measures can and cannot separate, not what the 135M model does.
