# Neural Garden v0.2 — Stewardship reward shaping on unchanged world laws

**Status:** Executed bounded comparative pilot, 2026-11-11 UTC.  
**Source:** `evaluate_stewardship.py`, complete records `stewardship_results.json`.  
**Reproduce:** `.venv/bin/python NEURAL_GARDEN_V01/evaluate_stewardship.py --output NEURAL_GARDEN_V01/stewardship_results.json` from the scratch project root.

## Question

Can changing only each cell's local learning reward to include resource preservation alter its behavior and energy trajectory, even when the physical world laws remain unchanged?

Three arms begin with identical seed-specific world and neural initial conditions:

- `v01_learning`: original prediction + energy-reward learning.
- `v02_stewardship`: **same physical transitions** and model architecture, but policy update uses `energy change + 0.65 × local resource change` as reinforcement signal.
- `v01_frozen`: same original world, no weight updates.

Five seeds **151, 163, 179, 191, 211**, distinct from the earlier v0.1 pilot and v0.1 extended follow-up, were frozen for this comparison before evaluation. Size 12×12, 144 independent neural cells, 600 ticks, with fixed checkpoints at 200 and 600. All outcomes are generated by policy-influenced trajectories; paired initial seeds do not imply identical subsequent world states. The stewardship coefficient 0.65 is a design choice, not calibrated or proved optimal.

## Results: means across five seeds

| Metric | Tick | v0.1 learned | v0.2 stewardship | v0.1 frozen |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mean cell energy | 200 | 0.01585 | **0.07465** | 0.05821 |
| Mean cell energy | 600 | 0.00876 | **0.05744** | 0.03233 |
| Fraction with energy > 0.1 | 200 | 0.28% | **24.17%** | 17.50% |
| Fraction with energy > 0.1 | 600 | 0% | **15.14%** | 2.78% |
| Harvest rate | 200 | 55.83% | 45.00% | 47.78% |
| Harvest rate | 600 | 62.78% | 38.75% | 47.50% |
| On-policy resource prediction MSE | 200 | 0.00393 | 0.00329 | 0.01524 |
| On-policy resource prediction MSE | 600 | **0.00186** | 0.00371 | 0.01483 |
| Standardized reference-action probe MSE | 600 | 0.03205 | 0.01666 | **0.01539** |

The stewardship reward resulted in less frequent harvesting and greater retained energy under the exact same physical laws. At 600 ticks, its mean energy was roughly 6.6 times v0.1 learning and 1.8 times frozen, but this is a five-seed, designed-reward result. The reward directly favors resource preservation; the behavior is **not spontaneous ecological ethics or self-organized social cooperation**. The original v0.1 learner still had the smallest **on-policy** resource MSE, illustrating that lower predictive error need not align with energy retention.

The standardized 50%-reference-action probe continues to favor frozen initial predictors over v0.2. Although stewardship narrows the v0.1 transfer gap, it does not establish improved out-of-distribution prediction. The separate common-world trajectory probe is available per seed in JSON; it asks a different transfer question and must not be merged with the standardized expectation score.

## Fundamental viability limit of the unchanged world laws

For a spatially homogeneous stationary population with mean harvest probability `p`, resource balance under the v0.1/v0.2 environmental laws gives

\[r^*(p)=\frac{0.06}{0.06+0.18p}.\]

The expected unclipped energy change per tick is

\[\Delta E^*(p)=0.25r^*(p)p-0.03-0.055p+0.008(1-p).\]

Its maximum over p in [0,1] is approximately **−0.00133** near p≈0.331. Thus no constant homogeneous harvest policy can maintain positive long-run energy under these specific laws. Spatial heterogeneity, temporal policies, and energy clipping can change finite-time dynamics; this mean-field argument is not a universal impossibility proof for every conceivable heterogeneous strategy. It does show why changing only the learning reward cannot be assumed to solve ecological viability.

Cells still act at zero energy; the simulator does **not** implement biological death, reproduction, population selection, or true survival. The `energy > 0.1` statistic is an **operational energy threshold**, not literal alive/dead classification.

## Conclusions and next discriminating comparison

1. V0.2 stewardship **improved energy retention** within this synthetic environment and horizon, by changing policy-learning incentives.
2. It did not create a viable ecosystem or demonstrate emergent cooperation.
3. On-policy performance and two independent prediction probes can disagree because their target distributions differ. Preserve all three separately.
4. A proposed v0.3 world-law variant with lower metabolism belongs in a **different intervention category**, not a reward-only improvement. Compare it against v0.2 on fresh seeds and do not pool its results into this same-law study.
5. Future controls should include an explicit non-neural conservation heuristic, matched-compute reward ablations, longer horizons, honest population turnover, and independent validation seeds. Preserve v0.1 and v0.2 unchanged as baselines.
